Choose a suitable sparring partner

A useful round depends on more than matching numbers on a scale. Talk with the coach about experience, goals, and the pace of the session.

Let the coach make the match

A coach can consider factors such as size, experience, skill level, and the purpose of the round. Tell them honestly how long you have trained, what you want to practise, and whether you have any concerns. Do not arrange a hard round with someone simply because they accepted a challenge.

Set boundaries together

Before starting, agree on the drill, intensity, and how either person can pause or stop. Keep the agreed pace throughout the round. If your partner increases the intensity, step back and get the coach’s attention rather than trying to match them.

Review the round

Afterward, share one thing that worked and one thing to improve. A good partner helps you practise with focus and respect. If the round stops being controlled or useful, tell the coach and end it.

Helpful questions before you start

  • What skill or drill is this round for?
  • What pace and contact level have we agreed on?
  • Who is supervising the round?
  • How can either person pause or stop?

England Boxing’s coaching guidance highlights supervision and suitable matching for experience, weight, age, and ability. Those are general coaching principles; follow the rules and safeguarding requirements that apply to your own club. View England Boxing’s coaching handbook.
